## Proposed manuscript architecture

Working title: **Sex Differences in Medicare Coverage, Payment, and Access: A Systematic Review**. Use this title only for the completed review; retain the preliminary label on the current white paper.

Budget the main text initially to approximately 3,700 words: introduction 450, methods 1,050, results 1,000, discussion 1,050 and conclusion 150. This is an internal drafting allocation. Confirm which material the journal counts before final submission; shorten if necessary.

Allocate the four main displays to (1) the real PRISMA flow; (2) included-study characteristics grouped by analytical stream; (3) outcome/effect and appraisal summary; and (4) the main policy/equity finding or clinical-need framework. This replaces, rather than adds to, the four illustrations in the preliminary white paper. Place exhaustive searches, extraction definitions, excluded reports, appraisal judgments and detailed policy tables in supplements as permitted by the journal. Do not shorten the methodological audit trail to meet main-text limits.

Keep original formulary and RVU analyses in a separately identified companion report initially. If editors prefer a combined article, each component needs its own transparent design and cannot borrow the systematic review's evidentiary status. Physician-gender findings remain explicitly targeted context unless a separate comprehensive review is performed.

## Practical process

1. **Settle scientific scope.** Review the attached protocol and identity-eligibility rule, appoint the human review team and confirm database access. Freeze the rules for the formal phase while documenting all earlier work.
2. **Check editorial fit early.** An optional presubmission inquiry can ask about Review Article suitability, the proposed separation of original CMS analyses, word-count exclusions and supplementary methods. A suggested inquiry is below. A favorable response would still not be acceptance.
3. **Execute the review.** Run and archive searches; independently screen; retrieve and extract full texts; appraise; synthesize; assess certainty where appropriate; update searches; reconcile the PRISMA flow and checklist.
4. **Build the submission package.** Manuscript/abstract/key takeaways, author/title information, cover letter, four displays, detailed methods and reporting supplements, reference library, data/code availability, funding/conflicts, contributions, actual ethics determination if applicable, and actual AI-use disclosure. Verify all authors' approval and budget/waiver arrangements.
5. **Submit and respond.** Complete Editorial Manager metadata and review the generated submission proof. Respond to requested revisions with a point-by-point letter and tracked/clean manuscripts as instructed. Publication/licensing arrangements follow editorial acceptance.

No step implies that these procedures have already occurred. We have not submitted the public white paper as a manuscript.

## Methods changes required before submission

- Replace selected source retrieval with reproducible, peer-reviewed multi-database searches and a search update.
- Re-screen the pilot; distinguish eligible Medicare empirical studies from clinical background and other-payer context.
- Obtain genuinely independent human screening, extraction and design-specific appraisal; AI checks do not satisfy reviewer independence.
- Resolve unreported gender identity under the existing scope. Do not relabel administrative sex as confirmed cisgender identity.
- Separate policy authority, effect estimates, causal claims and certainty; quantify clinical need without defining it through reimbursement eligibility.
- Use outcome-specific GRADE only when appropriate, with CERQual for an actual qualitative synthesis; avoid a single certainty rating for the entire heterogeneous register.
- Document previous exploratory extraction and hypotheses. Current PROSPERO rules require registration before extraction starts; do not promise prospective registration for this already-started work. A dated public protocol can transparently govern remaining work. [PROSPERO](https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/help/eligibility), [OSF](https://help.osf.io/article/330-welcome-to-registrations).

PRISMA 2020 supports transparent reporting, not a guarantee of complete searches, unbiased conduct, or publication [M-001; PMID 33782057]. The methods package also uses PRISMA-P, PRISMA-S, PRESS, PRISMA-Equity and appropriately scoped SWiM/CERQual guidance [M-003–M-008]. ## Optional presubmission inquiry — draft, not sent

Subject: Review Article inquiry — sex differences in Medicare coverage, payment and access

Dear Health Affairs Scholar Editors,

We are developing a systematic review of empirical evidence on sex-related differences in Medicare coverage, service valuation, patient costs and access to clinically indicated care. The proposed review separates shared-condition patient comparisons from reproductive-anatomy or physiology-specific services, and distinguishes patient equity from physician-gender payment differences. We intend to report the completed review using PRISMA 2020, with reproducible searches, independent human review, design-specific appraisal and detailed supplementary methods.

An exploratory white paper and selected descriptive CMS analyses are publicly available at https://github.com/clinical-trials/medicare. They are preliminary and are not a completed systematic review. We plan to keep the original CMS analyses separate unless a combined structure would better suit the journal.

Would this approach be suitable for consideration as a Review Article? We would also appreciate clarification of review word-count exclusions and the appropriate treatment of extensive methods supplements and companion CMS analyses.

The planned main text is approximately 3,700 words, with a structured abstract and no more than four main tables/figures. Author identities/affiliations, corresponding-author details, funding and disclosures must be supplied by the investigators before this inquiry is sent.

This draft has no author sign-off and has not been emailed. It is not a claim of journal interest or a completed submission.
