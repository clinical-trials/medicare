# Proposed manuscript Methods

September 16, 2026. **Future-tense working draft for the formal review. Not suitable for past-tense submission until the procedures are performed and actual dates, team roles, counts, tools and protocol identifiers are inserted.** The current white paper's completed-methods account remains valid for its exploratory stage. Full specifications and limitations are in [the protocol](Medicare_systematic_review_protocol.md).

## Design and eligibility

We will conduct a systematic review of empirical evidence concerning Medicare coverage, service valuation, patient costs and access to clinically indicated care. We will report the completed review using PRISMA 2020 and applicable PRISMA-S and PRISMA-Equity items [M-001, M-004, M-006]. A dated protocol will document prior exploratory searching, selected extraction, descriptive CMS analyses and hypothesis development; these earlier activities were not prospectively registered. Registration status and all subsequent amendments will be reported accurately.

The target population follows the investigator's September 16, 2026 scope amendment: cisgender women and men, including younger Medicare beneficiaries entitled through disability, ESRD or ALS. Transgender-focused patient studies and gender-affirming-care indications are outside this review's scope. Mixed populations or payers require separately extractable eligible findings. Administrative male/female codes do not establish cisgender identity. Unresolved identity eligibility will be recorded, investigated through full text and feasible clarification, and kept outside explicitly cisgender primary estimates unless the scope is transparently amended.

Eligible original studies will address coverage, reimbursement, patient spending, denial/delay, utilization or unmet need in Medicare, or identifiable Medicare code/service valuation. Clinical efficacy studies and other-payer evidence will be retained as labeled background where relevant. Patient-level evidence and service-valuation evidence will be synthesized separately. Physician-gender studies will provide targeted context and will not contribute to patient-sex estimates.

Primary patient evidence must include a relevant sex/gender comparison, a need-based assessment within a prespecified sex-linked indication or population, or differential sex-related need/access under a common policy. Generic unstratified Medicare utilization studies will not become eligible solely because they concern Medicare. Service studies must address a prespecified service/anatomy valuation question.

Incomplete clinical-need adjustment will be appraised rather than used to exclude otherwise relevant comparisons.

## Search and selection

We plan to search MEDLINE/PubMed, Embase, CINAHL, EconLit and a multidisciplinary citation index from inception to the final recorded search date, subject to documented database access. An information specialist other than the primary search author will peer-review the strategies using PRESS [M-005]. Core Medicare/payment/access searches will not require sex/gender terminology, allowing retrieval of sex-specific care and policy studies. Supplementary searches will address sex/gender, clinical indications, government empirical reports and backward/forward citations. We will report exact platforms, strategies, dates, limits, exports and deduplication. Clinical guidelines and official Medicare policies will have separate source and version logs.

Two human reviewers will independently screen records and full texts after piloting the eligibility rules. Disagreements will be reconciled, with third-reviewer adjudication where needed. We will retain unretrieved reports, exclusion reasons, multiple-report links and overlapping-cohort identifiers. Investigator-provided pilot papers will undergo the same eligibility review. Automation may assist organization, but will not substitute for independent human selection or appraisal.

## Extraction and appraisal

Using piloted forms, two reviewers will independently extract principal outcomes, denominators, estimates and subjective judgments. We will record verified citations/PMIDs, design, data period, Medicare component, population and identity measurement, clinical need, policy exposure, comparison, outcome definition, time horizon, effect estimates and uncertainty, covariates, missingness, funding and limitations. Each result will retain a source locator; corrections and related reports will remain linked.

Care will be classified as shared systems/functions, female or male reproductive anatomy, reproductive physiology, or mixed/site-dependent. Policy eligibility and disease burden will be recorded separately. Clinical need will be defined independently of reimbursement eligibility. For sex-specific services we will assess eligible-population need, alternatives and policy consistency without requiring an artificial opposite-sex comparator.

Two reviewers will apply design-appropriate risk-of-bias tools: RoB 2 for randomized intervention results, appropriate JBI tools for observational and qualitative designs, and ROBINS-I only for suitable nonrandomized intervention-effect questions. Tool versions and domain-level reasons will be reported. Policy authority and code-comparison validity will have separate transparent appraisal; they will not receive invented clinical-effect certainty scores.

## Synthesis and interpretation

We will group evidence by indication, clinical category, outcome, payer component and policy period, preserving null and contradictory findings. Work RVUs, total RVUs, actual payments, annual physician receipts, patient spending and receipt of appropriate care will remain distinct. Historical results will be interpreted against contemporaneous policy.

Structured synthesis will be the default. Meta-analysis will be considered only when independent studies share sufficiently compatible populations, comparisons, outcomes and estimands; model choices and sensitivity analyses will follow the detailed protocol. We will report effect sizes and uncertainty rather than count significant results. SWiM will inform non-meta-analytic intervention-effect synthesis where applicable [M-007]. GRADE will assess suitable outcome-specific bodies of effect evidence [M-002]; CERQual will assess findings from any formal qualitative synthesis [M-008]. Neither approach will be applied as a per-paper quality score.

Clinical need, possible confounding and pathways through benefit design or care delivery will guide interpretation. Observed differences will not automatically be labeled causal bias. Original CMS analyses, if included, will receive a separate methods subsection and reproducibility appendix and will remain distinct from the systematic evidence selection.

## Transparency

The final report will describe actual reviewer roles, ethics determination if required, funding, conflicts, author contributions, AI use, protocol deviations and data/code access. We will provide completed reporting checklists, a reconciled PRISMA flow, full searches, extraction definitions, appraisal judgments and a stable repository snapshot. No missing result counts, registration number or completed appraisal is asserted in this proposed text.
