## What the document is

The attachment is a 15-page open-access **narrative methodological guide**. It explains question frameworks, database searching, screening, extraction, protocol development, risk-of-bias tools, synthesis, meta-analysis, heterogeneity, sensitivity analysis, figures, and common errors. It is not a study of Medicare, sex/gender equity, drug coverage, procedures, or reimbursement. It does not report an original systematic-search flow, included-study set, risk-of-bias assessment, or synthesized clinical effect.

The document's guidance is therefore a source for workflow design. It cannot serve as evidence that estradiol, sildenafil, any procedure, or any Medicare rule is equitable or inequitable.

## Source instructions versus the investigator's request

The PDF uses instructional language for a general audience. Those statements are recommendations from the article's authors, not instructions that override the investigator's project scope. The operative request remains the Medicare review question in `REVIEW_SCOPE.md`: evaluate coverage, payment values, cost sharing, administrative requirements, and access for the defined population of cisgender women and men, while preserving uncertainty, men’s disadvantages, shared anatomy, reproductive anatomy/physiology, and the separate physician-gender stream.

The attachment does not change the existing exclusion of transgender-focused patient studies or gender-affirming-care indications. It also does not authorize a meta-analysis, establish that a comparison is clinically valid, or permit relabeling male/female administrative coding as confirmed cisgender identity.

## Useful guidance adopted for this project

The guide reinforces several procedures already specified in the submission protocol:

1. Define the question and eligibility rules before the formal search, using a framework suited to a mixed policy and access question rather than forcing every stream into a treatment PICO.
2. Search more than one relevant database, translate the strategy for each platform, and preserve exact queries, dates, limits, exports, and deduplication logs.
3. Use two independent human reviewers for title/abstract screening, full-text decisions, and key extraction judgments, with adjudication and documented reasons for exclusion.
4. Pilot and standardize extraction forms, preserve denominators and source locations, and avoid double counting reports from one cohort.
5. Select appraisal tools by design and estimand. Keep reporting guidance, risk-of-bias judgments, and certainty assessment separate.
6. Use narrative synthesis when studies are too heterogeneous for pooling, and perform sensitivity analyses only when the estimand and data support them.

## Important limitations and corrections for use

The guide is helpful but should not be followed mechanically:

- A rule such as “at least two databases” is not a completeness standard. Our review needs databases that cover biomedical, health-services, economics, and multidisciplinary evidence, with citation searching and official Medicare sources documented separately.
- Cochrane Library is mainly a source of reviews and controlled-trial records; it is not a replacement for all primary-study databases.
- The statement that meta-analysis is always part of a systematic review is too broad. Systematic reviews may use structured narrative synthesis without statistical pooling; meta-analysis can also be conducted without a systematic review, though that design is less defensible for this project.
- Funnel plots, Egger tests, and trim-and-fill are not routine solutions for publication bias. They require a sufficiently large, comparable set of studies and can be misleading in heterogeneous observational evidence.
- I² is a descriptive heterogeneity statistic, not a validity threshold or a reason by itself to select a fixed- or random-effects model.
- ROBINS-I and ROBINS-E are risk-of-bias tools for particular nonrandomized intervention or exposure questions. They are not generic reporting-bias tools and should not be applied to every cross-sectional payment table.
- GRADE assesses certainty for a body of evidence about an outcome; it is not an individual-paper quality score. STROBE and CONSORT are reporting guidelines, not risk-of-bias instruments. AMSTAR 2 appraises systematic reviews, not primary Medicare studies.
- The article's claim that meta-analysis is at the top of an evidence hierarchy should not be used to privilege a pooled estimate over a well-designed nonpooled analysis or a clinically important qualitative finding.

These corrections are why the formal protocol retains PRISMA 2020, PRISMA-P, PRISMA-S, PRESS, PRISMA-Equity, design-specific tools, SWiM, GRADE, and GRADE-CERQual as distinct sources.

## Application to the Medicare review

The attached guide is now used as secondary methodological background. It supports the operational protocol but does not change its status. The Medicare project still has **not** completed a comprehensive database search, independent human screening, final eligibility adjudication, formal risk-of-bias assessment, outcome-specific certainty assessment, or a completed PRISMA flow diagram. The current 34-record register remains a pilot evidence set.

The next formal phase should produce: (1) a librarian-reviewed search package; (2) immutable database exports and checksums; (3) independent screening and extraction logs; (4) design-specific appraisal tables; (5) separate patient, service-valuation, physician-gender, and policy evidence tables; and (6) a synthesis that pools only clinically and statistically compatible estimates.
