# Evidence-linked opportunities for research and implementation

Assessment date: **21 September 2026**. These six domains are **unranked hypotheses**, selected from the current register and supplementary intervention evidence. They are not findings of a completed systematic review, validated products, a market-size analysis or investment recommendations. No clinical inventory rows were added. [opportunities.json](opportunities.json) records each population, evidence link, intervention, outcomes, user/buyer hypothesis, workflow requirements, study design, alternatives and limitations. [opportunity_policy_sources.json](opportunity_policy_sources.json) records verified current policy context and dates.

| Domain | Clinically useful intervention to test | What the evidence permits |
|---|---|---|
| Prescription affordability/navigation | Couple patient-specific benefit information with pharmacist/clinician support that resolves the barrier and confirms receipt of appropriate treatment. | A bundled COPD program improved adherence; a real-time benefit-tool rollout had null average spending/fill effects. A new app's incremental benefit is unproven. |
| Post-fracture bone care | Detect qualifying fracture events, assign follow-up ownership and close the referral loop. | Men-only outreach improved testing, but post-randomization exclusions and absent fracture outcomes limit inference. Test contemporary fracture/function benefit in both sexes. |
| Non-drug prior authorization | Discover dated requirements, prepare traceable documentation and track responses with human review and fallback. | Current interoperability rules support workflow integration. Neither those rules nor the register establish patient benefit from a particular product. |
| Procedure-valuation research | Independently audit comparator choices, measure full-episode work and publish reproducible estimates, including null findings. | Broad anatomical, narrowly matched and time-normalized comparisons disagree. The immediate opportunity is better measurement; patient benefit would be indirect and separately tested. |
| Lymphedema item fulfillment | Connect the treating team, enrolled supplier and patient through fitting, receipt, use and problem resolution. | Earlier cost/coverage studies predate the current compression-item benefit. First establish current delivery and cost-sharing barriers. |
| Kidney education/support | Pair appropriate education referrals with patient-defined practical support and follow-up. | Clinician interviews identify possible barriers, not Medicare prevalence or intervention effectiveness. Begin with US patient/caregiver evidence. |

The medication proposal must preserve positive and null results. The COPD randomized invitation study **[L-T01; PMID 39073823]** increased proportion of days covered by **3.8 percentage points (95% CI 3.1–4.5)** in the intention-to-treat analysis; that bundled program did not isolate software, and exacerbation/total-spending estimates were not statistically significant. The real-time benefit implementation study **[L-T02; PMID 40608336]** had no statistically significant overall effect on patient out-of-pocket spending, total spending or fills. ([COPD trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39073823/), [RTBT implementation study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40608336/))

The post hoc 2026 study **[L-T03; PMID 42599729]** found a null overall Medicare fill-rate difference: **+0.5 percentage points (95% CI −2.4 to 3.4)**. Its Medicare high-cost subgroup estimate, **+10.5 points (1.9–19.2)**, is exploratory. Only 2.8% of all orders met availability/observation criteria, and the high-cost Medicare subgroup denominator was not provided in the supplement table. This report and parent **PMID 36094537** concern the same trial, **NCT04940988**. They are not independent trials or evidence that a product closes a sex gap. The mixed-payer +14.5-point subgroup result must not be labeled Medicare-specific. ([Post hoc report and supplements](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13476843/), [parent trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36094537/))

The fracture proposal is grounded in **[L-G05; PMID 33273290]**: BMD testing was **10.7% versus 4.9%**, but only **7,842 of 10,934 randomized patients** entered the analysis; fewer fractures were not demonstrated. It supports testing outreach, not automatic DXA for all men. USPSTF's 2025 screening recommendation excludes people with prior fragility fracture and finds insufficient screening evidence in men; that statement does not rule out post-fracture evaluation. Medicare's testing eligibility and frequency rules remain separate from clinical need. ([Outreach study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33273290/), [USPSTF scope](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/osteoporosis-screening), [Part B bone measurement coverage](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/bone-mass-measurements))

For valuation, retain **[L-P02; PMID 38758183]** as null evidence and **[L-P03; PMID 34736273]** as a metric-dependent result: female RVU/hour was higher, while modeled specialty compensation/hour was lower. The latter uses specialty compensation surveys, not Medicare conversion factors. Clinical purpose, anatomy, operative approach, full-episode work and patient need must be audited before pooling. ([L-P02](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38758183/), [L-P03](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8602770/))

Current policies create implementation conditions, not a business case:

- **Prior authorization:** CMS-0057-F has operational provisions generally beginning in **2026** and API requirements generally beginning in **2027** for specified impacted payers, including Medicare Advantage. Applicable non-drug decision timeframes are generally **72 hours expedited/seven calendar days standard**, with payer-specific exceptions; finalized timeframe provisions exclude FFE QHP issuers. The rule excludes drugs and does not make Original Medicare an impacted payer. CMS-0062-P, released **10 April 2026**, proposes drug extensions and other changes; it is treated here as a **proposal**, not a finalized mandate. ([Final rule](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/cms-interoperability-prior-authorization-final-rule-cms-0057-f), [current FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/burden-reduction/overview/interoperability/frequently-asked-questions/prior-authorization-api), [2026 proposal](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2026-cms-interoperability-standards-prior-authorization-drugs-proposed-rule))
- **Prescription benefits:** Part D sponsor prescriber RTBTs must use **NCPDP RTPB v13 from 1 January 2027**; beneficiary tools already have a separate requirement. HTI-4 certification milestones differ from payer obligations. The **2026 covered-Part-D out-of-pocket threshold is $2,100**; premiums/noncovered spending and patient cash flow require separate treatment. The Prescription Payment Plan spreads payments and **does not reduce drug prices or total drug costs**. ([CMS standards](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/regulations-guidance/electronic-prescribing/adopted-standard-and-transactions), [ONC certification](https://healthit.gov/resources/hti-4-overview-and-key-dates-for-certification-program/), [2026 benefit](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/final-cy-2026-part-d-redesign-program-instructions), [payment-plan explanation](https://www.medicare.gov/prescription-payment-plan/will-this-help-me))
- **Covered services versus software:** Compression-item furnishing/education is bundled in DMEPOS supplier payment; it does not create separate software payment. Kidney education coverage is limited to eligible stage-4 patients, referral/provider conditions and up to six sessions. Each proposed product's reimbursement and business model remains **UNVERIFIED**. ([Compression payment](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/fee-schedules/durable-medical-equipment-prosthetic-devices-prosthetics-orthotics-supplies/lymphedema-compression-treatment-items), [kidney education](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/kidney-disease-education))
- **Software function:** FDA's January 2026 CDS guidance requires evaluating actual intended functions; these proposals are not declared cleared, exempt or non-device. Documentation/navigation and diagnostic/treatment recommendations may have different regulatory treatment. ([FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software), [FDA FAQ](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/software-medical-device-samd/clinical-decision-support-software-frequently-asked-questions-faqs))

Clinical validation should compare against existing services, include people who never complete the workflow, and measure appropriate care, patient burden and harms. Buyer commitments, integration/support cost, applicable billing rules and sustainability require a separate assessment after the clinical/workflow question is answered. Recorded patient sex is not confirmed gender identity, and physician-gender payment studies do not supply patient-sex outcomes. No domain has demonstrated national sex-gap closure or commercial viability.
