# Material issues in the September 21 manuscript draft

Read-only AI scientific review of `manuscript.md` against the canonical register, targeted extraction, current protocol, formulary results, and current queue summary. Line references identify the draft inspected and may shift during editing. No manuscript edits made. The planned intervention-effects paragraph was not yet present and is outside this review.

1. **Incorrect physician specialty — line 101.** “Ophthalmologists, hospitalists and surgeons” misidentifies L-C02. Raber et al. studied **cardiologists**, not hospitalists. Replace “hospitalists” with “cardiologists.” Source: L-C02, *Gender Differences in Medicare Payments Among Cardiologists*, in `research/scoped_evidence.json` and its verified bibliography.

2. **Reversed publication chronology — line 89.** “A later comparison” describes L-P02 (Hathaway, 2024) after L-P01 (Penn, 2025). That chronology is wrong, and the L-P02 valuation-data year is unresolved. Use “another comparison” or name the publication years; do not imply the opposing finding reflects a subsequent policy change. Source: `research/review_2026-09-21/valuation/extraction.json` and verified L-P01/L-P02 citations.

3. **Service-valuation question narrows the adopted scope — line 39.** The second question limits services to those associated with reproductive anatomy or physiology. The adopted scope also covers shared-system services, including breast reconstruction and bone care, and forbids treating shared anatomy as reproductive merely because the population is predominantly women. Suggested replacement: “How are clinically relevant services valued under Medicare, including shared-system care and services linked to reproductive anatomy or physiology, relative to defensible clinical and resource comparators?” Source: `REVIEW_SCOPE.md`, clinical classification, and protocol streams A/B. This changes wording to match the existing scope rather than broadening eligibility.

4. **Study inclusion age versus policy eligibility — line 95.** For L-R03, “eligibility thresholds differed” is ambiguous in a reimbursement paper: these are the study's age-entry thresholds, not established Medicare coverage criteria or independently verified clinical-need thresholds. Say “study-entry age cutoffs differed: women aged at least 65 and men aged at least 70.” Continue to identify this as an abstract-only crude comparison. Source: L-R03 extraction in `research/scoped_evidence.json` and `research/review_2026-09-21/bone/`.

5. **Clinical efficacy is being carried into an intervention claim — line 83.** “These results support indication-specific decision support” follows L-D03/L-D04, which evaluate treatments, not a health-IT decision-support intervention. Replace with a bounded statement such as: “These findings could inform the clinical content of an indication-specific tool; whether such a tool improves decisions or access requires separate evaluation.” This preserves the proposed development idea without implying that clinical efficacy evidence validates software.

6. **Planned extraction is weaker than the adopted protocol — line 61.** “They will verify principal extractions” does not specify independent extraction and could be read as two reviewers checking a common AI draft. Protocol section 7 requires two reviewers to independently extract critical eligibility/outcome/denominator information and then reconcile. State that commitment explicitly, keeping performed AI preparation separate. Source: `research/submission/Medicare_systematic_review_protocol.md`, section 7; the current formulation does not falsely claim completion, but understates the promised safeguard.
