# Publication methods audit and proposed translation amendment

September 21, 2026. Independent AI audit of the current repository, not an independent human screening or appraisal. This document proposes publication-specific reporting and methods; it does not amend the adopted population scope or change the canonical evidence register.

## Publication designation and evidence boundaries

The defensible current designation is **“Preliminary evidence synthesis and translational research agenda.”** A suitable working title is *Medicare coverage, payment, and access equity: a preliminary evidence synthesis and research agenda*. The target remains a completed systematic review, but a completed systematic, scoping, or rapid review is not supported by the work performed. PRISMA is reporting guidance, not certification that the underlying review was conducted. [PRISMA 2020, M-001; PMID 33782057](https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020).

The most consequential correction concerns evidence roles. The working register contains **38 active records**, of which its dashboard labels 31 primary candidates and seven context. Those labels are not publication inclusion decisions: they include clinical efficacy trials, clinical reviews, non-Medicare studies, and unresolved payer or identity eligibility. The accompanying [record-by-record role proposal](evidence_roles.json) assigns all 38 records to provisional publication roles, retaining stable IDs and their original register status. No count here represents independently screened or finally included studies.

Keep four bodies of material distinct: empirical beneficiary coverage/cost/access studies; service valuation studies; physician-gender context; and clinical, policy, or other-payer background. Original CMS formulary and RVU analyses need their own methods and results, outside the literature-search flow. In particular, a drug efficacy study cannot establish coverage inequity; a specialty compensation multiplier cannot establish a Medicare payment rate; physician receipts cannot establish patient access; and a fee-schedule difference cannot establish its cause.

## Blocking methods work, in order

1. **Resolve scope before final eligibility decisions.** The current [scope](../../REVIEW_SCOPE.md) targets cisgender women and men. A pending investigator clarification concerns a separately labeled analysis of sex-recorded cohorts with unreported identity; no change has been adopted in this audit. Keep these cohorts unresolved under the present criterion. If the investigator authorizes an amendment, date it, preserve earlier decisions, define the separate estimand, and reapply it consistently. Do not infer identity from claims, anatomy, medication, or diagnosis. Do not apply a patient-identity test to code-only data that have no patient cohort.

2. **Finalize and peer-review the search.** Independent PRESS review has not occurred. The missed bone paper suggests a broader vocabulary problem, not merely a single paper to add. An information specialist should examine outcomes, subject headings, proximity/Boolean logic, and sensitivity across all body systems before translating the revised strategy. Search the other protocol-specified databases or explicitly amend their selection and document access limits. Conduct logged backward/forward citation searches and government research retrieval. The targeted health-IT extension needs its own defined purpose; it cannot establish a comprehensive intervention evidence base. PRESS identifies search-quality checks; it is not a substitute for completed searching. [PRESS, M-005; PMID 27005575](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27005575/).

3. **Complete study selection and reconcile routes.** Perform report-family deduplication, pilot the rules, and independently screen titles/abstracts and potentially eligible full texts as the current protocol requires. Re-screen all pilot-register sources; none is grandfathered into inclusion. Retain unavailable reports separately from assessed exclusions. Reconcile investigator-supplied, targeted, citation-search and database routes without double counting. Missing abstracts, missing numerical estimates, and null findings are not automatic exclusions. Cochrane guidance supports independent final inclusion decisions; duplicate title/abstract screening is this project's explicit commitment, not a claim that PRISMA universally mandates it. [Cochrane Handbook, chapter 4, section 4.6.4](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-04).

4. **Finish full-text extraction with independent critical-data review.** Verify payer, population, policy year, clinical need, observation window, outcome definition, numerator/denominator, confidence intervals, adjustment set, missing data, and cohort overlap. Keep measured sex, reported gender, physician gender, and service anatomy separate. Resolve the L-P02 ten-pair versus nine-pair companion discrepancy; verify L-P06 FECA inputs before labeling estimates Medicare; segregate L-P03 work RVUs from modeled specialty compensation; and check L-R01/L-R02 model denominators and reference coding before any pooling. Treat unresolved values as unresolved, not reconstructed facts. Independent extraction of outcome data and subjective judgments is supported by [Cochrane Handbook, chapter 5, sections 5.5.2–5.5.5](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-05).

5. **Appraise bias, comparability, and certainty.** Use design-appropriate tools with source-located judgments and reconciliation. Primary instrument references M-010 (RoB 2; PMID 31462531) and M-011 (revised JBI analytical cross-sectional tool; PMID 40521701) are now verified and imported; exact variants and implementation remain to be fixed and piloted. Apply RoB 2 to randomized intervention effects, not a sex subgroup association merely because its parent dataset came from a trial. Use an appropriate observational tool for exposure associations; apply ROBINS-I only when its intervention-effect target is applicable. Code/policy appraisal is a separate project framework. Assess certainty for an identified outcome/comparison/body of evidence when the framework fits, not as a paper-level score or a combined “bias” grade. The GRADE domains concern uncertainty in the effect or association; official policy authority alone does not demonstrate clinical equity. [Cochrane Handbook, chapter 14, section 14.2](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-14).

6. **Specify synthesis and denominators before estimating a national pattern.** Separate patient access, patient out-of-pocket spending, total drug expenditure, service RVUs, and provider receipts. Define need independently of coverage and include clinically eligible nonrecipients where data permit. Preserve under-65 entitlement and older-age strata; align historical outcomes with contemporaneous policy. Use clinically defensible service/resource comparisons, including postoperative work and setting. Shared anatomy and sex-linked physiology remain distinct. Retain null results, evidence of disadvantage to men, and cases where no opposite-sex comparator is meaningful. Pool only compatible independent estimands; do not count statistically significant papers or selected inventory rows as evidence of national bias. A narrative synthesis can be rigorous without a meta-analysis.

7. **Complete reporting and accountability.** Produce an actual PRISMA flow only after selection; provide study/report links, full-text reasons, search strategies, appraisal tables, outcome-specific certainty where appropriate, and auditable calculations. State the protocol's actual timing, all amendments, AI use, author contributions, funding, conflicts, and data permissions. No prospective registration claim can be recovered by backdating. Journal word/display limits determine presentation, not whether these steps may be omitted.

AI can continue substantial retrieval, extraction preparation, analysis, and drafting while human review is arranged; human review must not be simulated.

## Scope-preserving publication options

- **Now:** a preliminary synthesis/research agenda with explicit selection and certainty limits. It may present verified examples and exploratory hypotheses, but cannot make complete-review or validated-opportunity claims. Suitability for a journal remains an editorial decision.
- **Preferred final product:** a completed systematic review of the specified empirical Medicare questions, with a separately labeled translational assessment and separately reported original CMS analyses. The translational component must not select studies based on commercial appeal.
- **Alternative after a dated design decision:** a completed scoping review/evidence map if the primary objective becomes mapping questions, methods, and gaps across this heterogeneous field. This still requires systematic identification, selection, and charting; it is not a relabeling shortcut or evidence that interventions work. [JBI scoping guidance; PMID 33038124](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33038124/); [PRISMA-ScR; PMID 30178033](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30178033/).
- **Protocol or methods publication:** possible only with accurate disclosure of prior exploratory work and the receiving venue's eligibility. A public dated protocol/amendment is useful regardless of whether a protocol paper is accepted; it cannot make previous work prospective.

## Proposed methods amendment: translational opportunity assessment

**Status:** proposal prompted by the investigator's new translational aim on September 21, 2026, after preliminary evidence inspection. It is a project-specific structured assessment, not a validated opportunity scale, economic evaluation, market analysis, or new eligibility rule. It does not widen the patient population.

**Unit and separation.** Assess each documented care gap as a combination of eligible population, clinical indication/need, Medicare benefit and policy period, observed barrier, and proposed modifiable mechanism. Link each statement to study/policy IDs, source locators, effect estimates and denominators, uncertainty, and currentness. Keep observed gaps, plausible mechanisms, and product hypotheses in separate fields. Evidence of a gap does not establish the benefit of a proposed solution.

**Patient-benefit assessment first.** Specify whose appropriate care could improve, which patient-important outcome matters, and how an intervention could alter the barrier. Consider men, women, younger beneficiaries, and relevant social or disability strata within scope. Compare a technology proposal with usual care and realistic nontechnical alternatives, such as policy clarification, benefit redesign, or staffing. Do not assume higher service volume is beneficial. Capture patient preferences, clinical appropriateness, avoidable costs, and possible harms such as incorrect coverage advice, inappropriate treatment, delayed care, increased administrative burden, and unequal digital access.

**Evidence for the solution.** Search and appraise intervention evidence separately before claiming effectiveness. The 262-hit prescription/health-IT extension is a development search, not this completed assessment. Identify whether evidence concerns feasibility, clinician behavior, patient cost/access, health outcomes, or distributional effects. Explicitly distinguish Medicare-specific evidence from other settings. Do not transfer a cost-effectiveness or equity claim from a disease burden paper to an untested product.

**Testing specification.** For each proposal, define a falsifiable mechanism, comparator, eligibility independent of existing receipt, follow-up, and denominators. Prespecify patient benefit, harm, appropriate-care, affordability, and equity outcomes along with implementation outcomes. A prescribing prompt, for example, may improve completion while failing to reduce out-of-pocket burden; report both. Avoid selecting only tool users, completed prescriptions, successful appeals, or paid claims when evaluating access. Describe how missingness, nonuse, displacement of work, and unequal reach would be measured.

**Feasibility and commercial assessment separately.** Document data provenance, coverage/indication mapping, update frequency, missingness, interoperability, workflow responsibility, auditability, and human correction. In a separate section identify potential adopters and buyers, payment/procurement routes, existing alternatives, development/operating costs, incentive conflicts, and unknowns. These observations neither prove patient benefit nor constitute market size. Do not derive a total addressable market from aggregate Medicare spending, the number of formulary listings, or the selected inventory. Do not combine benefit, equity, evidence certainty, or revenue into arbitrary weighted scores.

**Decision output.** Use transparent unmet prerequisites: “requires problem validation,” “specified for a feasibility study,” or “specified for a comparative benefit study,” with an explicit explanation and unresolved conditions. These are project planning states, not validated rankings or adoption recommendations. A study-ready designation requires a defined eligible population, plausible modifiable mechanism, accessible data, measurable benefit/harms, and an appropriate comparator; it does not establish commercial viability. Final prioritization should record patient/clinical input and conflicts when that work actually occurs.

Minimum extraction fields are implemented in [readiness.json](readiness.json). The eventual manuscript should distinguish a research priority, an implementation hypothesis, and a commercially viable venture; current evidence can support the first two only to the extent that the supporting data and uncertainties are documented.
