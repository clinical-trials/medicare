# Supplemental methods and publication completion checklist

September 21, 2026. Supplement to the preliminary evidence synthesis, *Sex related differences in Medicare care and health IT opportunities*. This account separates work performed from methods proposed for the unfinished systematic review. It does not amend population eligibility or claim registration, completed human review, formal appraisal, or a completed systematic search across databases.

## S1. Design, scope, and analytical roles

The adopted [review scope](../../REVIEW_SCOPE.md) addresses nationwide Medicare coverage, payment, patient costs, and access to clinically indicated care for cisgender women and men. Transgender-focused patient studies and gender-affirming indications are outside the current scope. Administrative male/female coding does not establish gender identity. Identity-unreported cohorts remain unresolved candidates and cannot support a confirmed-cisgender primary estimate. Investigator clarification about a separately labeled sex-recorded analysis is pending. Any change requires a dated amendment, a specified estimand, and consistent reconsideration of affected records; other work can continue under the existing criterion.

Patient comparisons, service/anatomy valuation, physician-gender context, and clinical/policy background are separate analytical streams. Shared systems/functions, reproductive anatomy, reproductive physiology, and mixed/site-dependent services retain their existing classification. Clinical need, disease burden, and sex-defined policy eligibility are distinct fields. Younger Medicare beneficiaries remain within scope; age, entitlement, payer component, indication, setting, and policy period are retained where available. Clinically eligible nonrecipients are relevant to access denominators, and lower observed use alone does not establish unmet need.

The active register contains 38 candidate or contextual records, not 38 systematically selected studies. A publication-role audit covers all 38 in [evidence_roles.json](evidence_roles.json). Four additional intervention reports, L-T01, L-T02, L-T03 and L-T03-P1, are translational context outside that active register. The current scientific metadata set totals 53 records: 38 active, eight earlier scope exclusions, one linked Lancet correction, two outside-register leads/context records, and four intervention reports. L-T03 and L-T03-P1 describe the same trial, not two independent trials. The nine hypotheses and six opportunity domains were developed after preliminary evidence inspection and remain exploratory.

Eleven methodological references are retained separately in the canonical methodology register. The newly verified primary instrument references M-010 and M-011 do not increase the scientific-study count and do not indicate that appraisal has been completed.

This repair is a diagnostic observation, not a validated recall estimate. No language, age, design, or full-text filter was applied. Date partitions were technical retrieval steps for ESearch's output limit, not substantive date exclusions.

## S3. Record management and review independence

Exact-PMID deduplication is complete across the documented development queries. Seventeen shared-DOI groups and 303 normalized-title groups have been flagged, with no automatic merges; the groups may overlap and are not additional known duplicate counts. Reviewers must distinguish bibliographic duplicates, corrections, multiple reports, follow-up analyses and overlapping cohorts. Secondary trial reports retain their own report IDs and are linked to a study family. Companion results can inform different outcomes but must not contribute the same participants twice to a pooled comparison.

Fifty purposively sampled calibration records have been prepared. Human decisions remain blank; the independent human screening count is zero. Selected AI extraction, source-location checks and numerical validation have occurred, with access limits documented. They do not constitute two human reviewers or completed full-corpus selection.

The current protocol plans two human reviewers independently screening titles/abstracts and full texts, followed by reconciliation and third-person adjudication when needed. The calibration plan includes approximately 50 records and ten full texts. Potentially eligible reports not retrieved will be reported separately from assessed full texts excluded, with a defined primary-reason hierarchy. Existing pilot records are subject to the same final rules as newly identified records. Missing usable numerical data can prevent pooling without necessarily making an otherwise eligible study ineligible.

Critical eligibility judgments, outcome data, denominators and appraisal judgments will be independently extracted before reconciliation, retaining individual and consensus records. Bibliographic fields may be extracted once and verified by another reviewer as specified in the protocol. Full reports and relevant companion material, not abstracts alone, are needed for final methodological assessment. Sources with unresolved discrepancies remain flagged. AI assistance will be described by model/tool, task, date, procedure, human verification and limitations. These are the project's present commitments; this supplement does not imply that PRISMA universally requires exactly this staffing model.

Specific unresolved source issues are preserved in the [intervention extraction log](intervention_search_log.md). L-T03's main text and supplement differ on follow-up end dates, age-band labels, and excluded-order counts. Its intermediate-cost Medicare confidence interval appears inconsistently printed and was not silently corrected or used in the manuscript. The overall and highest-cost Medicare estimates were legible and separately extracted, but the highest-cost subgroup lacks a reported Medicare denominator, is exploratory, and comes from a small selected subset of trial orders. Its parent report is the same trial. These issues require reconciliation against the original protocol and, where needed, authorized author clarification before interpreting the subgroup as definitive. The L-T01 COPD report also contains a demographic-table arithmetic inconsistency, retained without inventing a corrected value. These are source discrepancies, not evidence of misconduct; uncertain fields remain explicit. No clarification request has been sent.

## S4. Appraisal and synthesis: planned methods

Formal risk-of-bias and certainty assessments have not been completed. Reviewers will select instruments by design and the result being interpreted. RoB 2 addresses randomized intervention effects; its parallel-group, cluster and crossover variants must not be treated interchangeably. An observational sex association from an RCT-derived cohort is not automatically a randomized contrast. The revised JBI analytical cross-sectional instrument addresses that design's exposure/outcome associations and does not substitute for cohort, prevalence, qualitative, quasi-experimental, or policy appraisal. Exact applicable versions, manuals, interpretation rules and pilot decisions must be documented before appraisal. The primary sources are Sterne et al. [M-010; PMID 31462531](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31462531/), and Barker et al. [M-011; PMID 40521701](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40521701/). Their bibliographic verification is not evidence that either tool has been applied.

Policy authority, effective dates, coding completeness, matching, setting and resources receive a separately described project appraisal. Work RVUs, total RVUs, allowed amounts, actual payments, provider receipts, total drug expenditure and patient out-of-pocket costs are different outcomes. Public formulary listings do not establish drug suitability, paid claims, affordability or unmet need. Original CMS analyses remain reproducible companion analyses, outside the literature's study-selection flow.

Structured synthesis by analytical stream, clinical indication, outcome, payer component and policy period is the default. The following **conditions for future pooling** operationalize the existing protocol; they do not retroactively make the nine current hypotheses prospective:

1. Establish a common question and estimand, including population, clinical need, exposure/intervention, comparator, outcome and time horizon, before selecting estimates by their results.
2. Confirm independent participant/study contributions or explicitly model dependence; reconcile report families and overlapping Medicare claims periods.
3. Establish compatible designs, measurement definitions and units. Keep randomized assignment effects, observational associations, modeled compensation and descriptive valuation separate. Distinguish medication possession, actual fills, clinical outcomes and patient costs. Do not combine odds ratios with risk ratios, or dollar spending with RVUs, without a justified transformation and common estimand.
4. Verify denominators, uncertainty and adjustment sets. Analyze unadjusted contrasts and appropriately adjusted effects according to their distinct causal interpretations; do not select the most adjusted or most significant result automatically. Treat possible mechanisms separately from confounders.
5. Obtain methods/statistical review of the planned model. For a compatible group, the existing protocol proposes random effects with REML and suitable small-sample inference; the precise method must be finalized before pooling. Report weights, uncertainty, heterogeneity and meaningful prediction intervals when estimable. Neither two studies nor a low heterogeneity statistic alone justifies pooling.
6. Prespecify sensitivity analyses for risk of bias, cohort overlap, policy period, payer directness and publication status. Subgroups require suitable data and direct interaction assessment where appropriate; separate within-group significance is not an interaction test. Post hoc subgroup findings remain exploratory.

Where pooling is inappropriate, present effect estimates, uncertainty, direction, clinical applicability and risk-of-bias limitations without vote counting statistically significant results. SWiM [M-007] is used where its intervention-effect scope fits, with any adaptation explicitly described. Lack of an asymmetry test does not establish absence of selective reporting.

GRADE [M-002] applies to suitable outcome/comparison bodies of evidence; it is not a numerical study score or a rating for every statute, inventory row or procedure code. The methodological approach must fit the effect/association question. GRADE-CERQual [M-008] concerns confidence in findings from an actual qualitative evidence synthesis, not a grading label assigned to a single interview paper. Descriptive maps and non-effect valuation summaries should state source confidence, comparability and measurement limits without mislabeling them formal GRADE ratings. No risk-of-bias, certainty or opportunity score is inferred from study size or statistical significance.

## S5 Reporting extensions

PRISMA-S is already retained as **M-004**, Rethlefsen et al., [PMID 33499930](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33499930/); a duplicate citation is unnecessary. Final reporting will identify each database and platform, execution date, exact strategy/limits, supplementary route, peer review, updates, per-source counts, exports and deduplication procedure, with links to stable archived material. These reporting fields do not establish search completeness. PRISMA 2020 [M-001] and PRISMA-Equity [M-006] remain the existing reporting frameworks.
