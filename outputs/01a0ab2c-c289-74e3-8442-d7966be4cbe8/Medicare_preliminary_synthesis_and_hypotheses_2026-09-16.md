# Medicare sex/gender equity: preliminary synthesis and hypotheses

Version 1.1 | 2026-09-21 | Preliminary narrative synthesis and exploratory hypotheses; no causal tests completed.

**Working thesis:** Medicare may produce sex-related inequities when benefit rules, service valuation and practical access do not align with clinical need. The direction and mechanism may differ across conditions; a uniform disadvantage to one sex is not established.

Structured narrative synthesis of the selected active register and previously extracted CMS data. Organize by unit, clinical indication, policy period and outcome. Retain contradictory findings and distinguish observed patterns from proposed mechanisms. No meta-analysis, formal certainty grade or nationwide prevalence estimate of bias is attempted. The source set contains 40 care-item rows and 38 active literature records. The hypothesis numbers below are research propositions, not additional studies.

The organizing clinical review is [L-B01; PMID 32828189](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32828189/); retain its correction [L-B01-E1; PMID 32891210](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32891210/). It supports a framework for clinical need and sex/gender mechanisms, rather than a direct Medicare payment conclusion.

## Patterns in the current evidence

### PAT-01. Coverage depends on formulation and indication

**Observed:** In the existing August 2026 CMS snapshot, oral and vaginal-cream generic estradiol have a listed option without PA/ST in every analyzed segment; other formulations and products vary. Basic Part D excludes drugs when used for sexual or erectile dysfunction, with different rules for other eligible indications.

**Interpretation to investigate:** Investigate clinically suitable options and actual access within each indication. The original estradiol-versus-Viagra premise does not establish a national female disadvantage.

**Limit:** Listing is not a paid claim or affordable care. Molecules, indications and routes are not interchangeable. Exclusion for sexual dysfunction concerns both male- and female-directed treatments; supplemental benefits differ.

Sources: [L-D01; PMID 38527966](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38527966/); [L-D03; PMID 29554173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29554173/); [L-D04; PMID 39250810](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39250810/); [P-002](https://data.cms.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/d8c9b393-66f0-4973-a748-f66742fe0fd2/2026_20260819.zip); [P-003](https://www.ssa.gov/OP_Home/ssact/title18/1860D-02.htm); [P-026](https://www.cms.gov/Medicare/Prescription-Drug-Coverage/PrescriptionDrugCovContra/Downloads/QAEDDrugs_070606.pdf); [P-034](https://www.cms.gov/files/zip/cy-2026-august-formulary-reference-file.zip); [P-037](https://data.cms.gov/provider-summary-by-type-of-service/medicare-part-d-prescribers/monthly-prescription-drug-plan-formulary-and-pharmacy-network-information); [P-038](https://data.cms.gov/sites/default/files/2025-10/0564eb37-402d-4110-bd98-2d5399dc30e7/PUFRecordLayout-2026.pdf).

### PAT-02. Procedure valuation findings are sensitive to clinical matching and the payment measure

**Observed:** Penn reports lower work RVUs for 41 of 55 female-anatomy procedures; Hathaway finds no significant overall difference in 10 closely matched pairs. Polan reports higher female-procedure RVUs/hour but lower modeled specialty compensation/hour. The existing five-pair 2026 check contains differences in both directions.

**Interpretation to investigate:** A conditional valuation problem is plausible and merits a prespecified comparison of clinically comparable work. Specialty compensation, work RVUs and actual Medicare payments require separate analyses.

**Limit:** Selected pairs cannot establish a universal effect. Nonsignificance does not establish equivalence. Full texts of Penn/Hathaway remain pending; Hathaway journal/companion pair counts disagree (10/9). Polan dollar rates model specialty compensation; some printed arithmetic needs verification.

Sources: [L-P01; PMID 39978776](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39978776/); [L-P02; PMID 38758183](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38758183/); [L-P03; PMID 34736273](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34736273/); [L-P04; PMID 34237766](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34237766/); [L-P05; PMID 9062137](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9062137/); [L-P06; PMID 28024653](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28024653/); [P-035](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/fee-schedules/physician/pfs-relative-value-files/rvu26c); [P-036](https://auanews.net/issues/articles/2024/july-extra-2024/upj-insight-comparison-of-work-relative-value-units-assigned-to-urological-and-gynecological-surgical-procedures).

### PAT-03. Benefit design can matter within a population without a male-female counterpart

**Observed:** Among younger women with disabilities, gaining dual Medicare-Medicaid enrollment was associated with a 3.9-percentage-point rise in contraceptive use. Separate studies describe spending burdens after breast cancer and limited insurer coverage for lymphedema services.

**Interpretation to investigate:** Study whether clinically indicated, desired care is obtained after benefit changes. Sex-specific care does not need an opposite-sex counterpart to be evaluated for equitable access.

**Limit:** Insurance transitions are observational. More contraceptive use is not automatically better care; patient preference matters. Lymphedema studies predate the 2024 Medicare compression benefit and do not describe all current Medicare services.

Sources: [L-G09; PMID 40560583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40560583/); [L-G10; PMID 38190592](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38190592/); [L-A01; PMID 31446591](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31446591/); [L-A02; PMID 37285151](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37285151/); [P-021](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/lymphedema-compression-treatment-items); [P-022](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/regulations-guidance/transmittals/2024-transmittals/r12532bp).

### PAT-04. Disadvantage can affect men in shared conditions

**Observed:** Historical Medicare bone-testing use was lower in men. A men's fragility-fracture outreach program increased testing from 4.9% to 10.7%. A newly checked, identity-unresolved Asian American Medicare study reports DXA receipt of 19.8% in women and 5.0% in men, with different age thresholds. A separate women-only MA study reports lower two-year bone measurement at age >=80 than 65–79.

**Interpretation to investigate:** Test gaps in appropriate care in either direction. Clinical recognition, referral and treatment completion may matter alongside reimbursement.

**Limit:** New patient records have unresolved identity eligibility. The Asian American result is crude and abstract-only, with women >=65 versus men >=70; it is not a national adjusted sex effect. MA women-only results do not estimate a male/female gap. Need, preferences, intervals and delivery barriers remain distinct from coverage.

Sources: [L-G04; PMID 18302495](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18302495/); [L-G05; PMID 33273290](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33273290/); [L-R03; PMID 38459983](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38459983/); [L-R04; PMID 27884649](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27884649/); [P-002](https://data.cms.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/d8c9b393-66f0-4973-a748-f66742fe0fd2/2026_20260819.zip); [P-012](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/bone-mass-measurements).

### PAT-05. Physician annual-payment gaps are not equivalent to per-service rate gaps

**Observed:** Women physicians had lower annual Medicare collections/payments in the ophthalmology, cardiology and surgery studies. Cardiology common-code comparisons and colorectal payment-per-service results include null findings.

**Interpretation to investigate:** Referral opportunity, service mix, clinical time and billing circumstances are competing mechanisms to investigate in a separate physician evidence stream.

**Limit:** Payments are not salary. Complete modifiers and setting are needed for comparable-rate tests. These studies do not estimate patient-sex coverage differences or establish the cause of annual gaps.

Sources: [L-C01; PMID 31926887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31926887/); [L-C02; PMID 34495296](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34495296/); [L-C03; PMID 39046733](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39046733/).

### PAT-06. Clinical need and practical barriers connect the evidence but remain incompletely measured

**Observed:** The Lancet review connects biology and social pathways to clinical need; kidney-care interviews identify perceived practical barriers. Newly extracted, identity-unresolved Medicare studies report female-higher cost-related nonadherence in a 2016 national survey but an imprecise adjusted persistent-nonadherence contrast in a selected Chicago cohort.

**Interpretation to investigate:** A useful research model separates clinical need, recognition/referral, eligibility, financial/administrative burden and completed care. It permits both policy and nonpolicy mechanisms.

**Limit:** Patient identity eligibility and human verification remain pending. Annual any-nonadherence and persistent nonadherence are different outcomes; do not pool them. These historical observational results do not establish current coverage discrimination.

Sources: [L-B01; PMID 32828189](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32828189/); [L-B01-E1; PMID 32891210](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32891210/); [L-Q01; PMID 35257055](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35257055/); [L-P07; PMID 42085599](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42085599/); [L-P08; PMID 41701182](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41701182/); [L-P09; PMID 32453268](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32453268/); [L-R01; PMID 33298705](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33298705/); [L-R02; PMID 33656528](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33656528/).

## Testable hypotheses

All hypotheses were developed after viewing this evidence. Priorities describe the proposed research sequence, not certainty or importance scores. The outcome definitions, effect sizes of interest, windows and adjustment sets must be finalized before confirmatory testing.

### HYP-01. Formulation-specific restrictions and unmet menopausal care

**Patient care/access. Hypothesis:** Among beneficiaries with an independently established need for a particular menopausal/GSM formulation, higher financial or authorization burdens reduce timely initiation and persistence with clinically suitable treatment.

**Population:** Clinically eligible Medicare beneficiaries seeking GSM or menopausal symptom treatment; stratify age, indication and route. Assess contraindications and informed preferences.

**Exposure and comparison:** Formulary nonlisting, PA/ST and actual out-of-pocket burden; analyze each separately. Same indication and clinical requirements under less restrictive plans or a verified policy change. Do not assume oral, local and transdermal treatments are interchangeable.

**Outcome:** Prespecified primary: timely initiation of an appropriate desired therapy. Secondary: abandonment, persistence, delays, symptoms and patient spending.

**Proposed test:** First audit options within each plan and indication. Then use linked prescription orders, claims, denials and benefit data to compare policy changes with a defensible control and pretrend checks; account for plan selection and formulary clustering.

**Alternative explanations:** Clinical contraindications, efficacy and safety differences, generic substitution, negotiated prices, patient preference, prescribing practice and healthy-user selection.

**What would weaken it:** Precise estimates excluding a meaningful initiation gap after suitable alternatives, preferences and clinical differences are measured would weaken this mechanism. A nonsignificant imprecise estimate would not.

**Readiness:** Descriptive formulation audit ready; patient-access test needs linked data. **Priority:** First analysis. **Next step:** Use the three-state formulary summary, then inspect exact strengths/routes and clinical eligibility. Obtain unique-formulary and enrollment-weighted comparisons before estimating beneficiary exposure.

Motivating evidence: [L-D01; PMID 38527966](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38527966/); [L-D04; PMID 39250810](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39250810/). Counterevidence or qualification: [L-D03; PMID 29554173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29554173/); [L-D10; PMID 12117397](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12117397/). ### HYP-02. Female-anatomy valuation after comparable work is measured

**Service/anatomy valuation. Hypothesis:** Across a prespecified set of clinically analogous procedures, female-anatomy procedures receive lower average work RVUs after independent measurement of complete physician work.

**Population:** A prespecified universe of clinically analogous procedures; services without a counterpart remain eligible for separate need/value assessment.

**Exposure and comparison:** Female-anatomy versus male-anatomy procedure category, not physician gender. Pairs matched on clinical purpose, setting, complexity, global period and independently measured episode work within the same fee-schedule year.

**Outcome:** Primary: mean paired work-RVU difference across the prespecified set. Secondary: fraction exceeding a prespecified meaningful disadvantage and standardized allowed payment, with work, practice-expense and malpractice components separate.

**Proposed test:** Define pairs before examining payment direction. Extract the full published pair sets into the same current CMS release; assess pair-selection sensitivity and complete pre-, intra- and postoperative work. Avoid treating current RUC assumptions as independent proof of equal work.

**Alternative explanations:** Different technical intensity, risk, perioperative work, bundling, code revisions and pair selection; specialty compensation may change modeled dollars without changing the Medicare conversion factor.

**What would weaken it:** Precise estimates ruling out the prespecified meaningful mean female disadvantage across defensible matching definitions would weaken the average-effect claim. Report heterogeneous and reversed pairs separately; average parity cannot establish parity for every procedure.

**Readiness:** Five-pair descriptive check exists; full pair extraction and work measures needed. **Priority:** First analysis. **Next step:** Extend the current five-pair check to complete prespecified published sets. Audit code meaning and global/setting compatibility; report all directions before considering pooling.

Motivating evidence: [L-P01; PMID 39978776](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39978776/); [L-P04; PMID 34237766](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34237766/); [L-P05; PMID 9062137](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9062137/); [L-P06; PMID 28024653](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28024653/). Counterevidence or qualification: [L-P02; PMID 38758183](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38758183/); [L-P03; PMID 34736273](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34736273/). Policy/data: [P-035](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/fee-schedules/physician/pfs-relative-value-files/rvu26c); [P-036](https://auanews.net/issues/articles/2024/july-extra-2024/upj-insight-comparison-of-work-relative-value-units-assigned-to-urological-and-gynecological-surgical-procedures).

### HYP-03. Contraceptive benefits and desired care for younger beneficiaries

**Patient care/access. Hypothesis:** More complete contraceptive benefits increase receipt of desired, clinically appropriate contraception among younger Medicare beneficiaries with disabilities, especially when financial constraints are substantial.

**Population:** Eligible reproductive-age Medicare beneficiaries with disabilities; identify clinical need and pregnancy intentions rather than using enrollment alone.

**Exposure and comparison:** Verified benefit expansion or gaining relevant supplemental/Medicaid benefits, with actual cost sharing documented. Comparable beneficiaries without the benefit change and the same individuals before the change; no forced male comparator.

**Outcome:** Primary: receipt of the patient-preferred appropriate method among those desiring it. Secondary: cost-related nonuse, delays and switching.

**Proposed test:** Replicate the coverage-transition association with longitudinal eligibility and claims, assess pretrends and reasons for transitions, and add patient-reported preferences. Distinguish increased availability from coercive or unwanted use.

**Alternative explanations:** Changing income, disability/health status, pregnancy intentions, provider networks, state Medicaid rules and cash purchases missed in claims.

**What would weaken it:** No improvement in need- and preference-concordant access with sufficiently precise estimates after a verified benefit expansion would weaken this hypothesis; utilization alone is insufficient.

**Readiness:** Published association available; causal replication needs new data. **Priority:** Requires new data. **Next step:** Extract study eligibility and policy periods fully, then seek longitudinal benefit and preference data. WPS IUD guidance is local, the sterilization NCD is national, and Part D drug coverage requires product/plan review. Preserve younger disability beneficiaries separately.

Motivating evidence: [L-G09; PMID 40560583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40560583/); [L-G10; PMID 38190592](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38190592/). Policy/data: [P-015](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/article.aspx?articleid=55951); [P-018](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/ncd.aspx?ncdid=13); [P-019](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/prescription-drugs-outpatient).

### HYP-04. A male access gap after fragility fracture

**Patient care/access. Hypothesis:** Among women and men with comparable fracture-related osteoporosis care needs, men receive less appropriate follow-up, and structured outreach reduces that gap.

**Population:** Medicare beneficiaries after comparable fragility fractures; assess fracture type, prior testing/treatment, prognosis, contraindications and preferences.

**Exposure and comparison:** Recorded patient sex and a separately assigned outreach intervention; preserve uncertainty about gender identity. Women versus men within clinical-need and age/entitlement strata; outreach versus usual care within each sex.

**Outcome:** Primary: guideline-concordant completed osteoporosis evaluation/treatment pathway. Report referral, testing, medication offer and initiation separately.

**Proposed test:** Use a clinically characterized cohort including nonrecipients, then a pragmatic outreach trial with a prespecified patient-sex interaction. Separate current benefit eligibility from independently assessed need.

**Alternative explanations:** Different fracture risk/severity, survival, prior treatment, contraindications, clinical recognition, patient preferences and access to follow-up.

**What would weaken it:** Comparable need-adjusted completion in women and men would weaken the access-gap claim. Similar outreach effects with a persistent gap would not support the proposed gap-reduction mechanism.

**Readiness:** Historical comparison and male outreach study available; current joint test needs data. **Priority:** Priority patient study. **Next step:** Fully extract the male outreach trial, including post-randomization exclusions, and define contemporary need-based outcomes without making testing alone the goal.

Motivating evidence: [L-G04; PMID 18302495](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18302495/); [L-G05; PMID 33273290](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33273290/). Policy/data: [P-012](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/bone-mass-measurements).

### HYP-05. Unequal burden under formally similar access requirements

**Patient care/access. Hypothesis:** Among beneficiaries with comparable kidney-care needs and nominal coverage, financial, transport and caregiving demands contribute to sex differences in completing recommended care.

**Population:** Women and men referred for comparable kidney-care pathways; stratify disease severity, age and entitlement.

**Exposure and comparison:** Measured financial, travel, time and caregiving burden under the same coverage rules; patient and physician sex/gender recorded separately. Beneficiaries with comparable need but lower practical burden; evaluate targeted support versus usual care within sex strata.

**Outcome:** Primary: completion of clinically appropriate, desired care within a prespecified interval. Secondary: delay, out-of-pocket burden and patient-reported unmet need.

**Proposed test:** Collect referral, treatment-offer, attendance, burden and preference data prospectively. Test effective transport/caregiving support with a prespecified patient-sex interaction; separate total disparity from exploratory mediation.

**Alternative explanations:** Disease trajectory, medical contraindications, geography, provider supply, unconstrained preferences and differences in treatment offers.

**What would weaken it:** Precise evidence of comparable need-adjusted completion and burdens across sexes would weaken the proposed disparity. If support demonstrably reduces the between-sex burden difference but precisely fails to reduce the corresponding completion gap, the mediation hypothesis is weakened. Absence of an interaction alone is inconclusive.

**Readiness:** Qualitative mechanism only; requires patient-level prospective evidence. **Priority:** Exploratory mechanism. **Next step:** Retrieve full qualitative methods and contradictory accounts. Develop measures with patients; do not treat clinician perceptions as measured Medicare effects.

Motivating evidence: [L-Q01; PMID 35257055](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35257055/); [L-B01; PMID 32828189](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32828189/).

### HYP-06. Physician opportunity and annual Medicare payments

**Physician sex/gender. Hypothesis:** Differences in referral opportunities, access to procedures and clinical time contribute to annual Medicare payment differences between women and men physicians.

**Population:** Physicians with recorded specialty, practice, career stage and clinical commitments. Document clinician sex/gender measurement; treat clinical time as a possible mechanism rather than automatically conditioning the primary comparison on it.

**Exposure and comparison:** Physician sex/gender and measured referral/procedure opportunities, distinct from patient sex. Women and men physicians in comparable practice contexts; separately compare complete claims for comparable services.

**Outcome:** Primary: total annual Medicare payment difference within the prespecified population. Secondary: payments standardized to measured clinical time, referral/procedure opportunities, service mix, and payment per complete comparable claim.

**Proposed test:** Link practice rosters, scheduled clinical time, referrals and claims. Estimate annual differences first, then explore opportunity/service pathways without automatically adjusting away mechanisms. Test rates using modifiers, setting, geography and all relevant billing details.

**Alternative explanations:** Scheduling preferences, Medicare share, case mix, practice ownership, coding, employment arrangements and work not reimbursed by Medicare.

**What would weaken it:** Equal measured opportunities with persistent annual differences would weaken that mechanism. Equivalent complete-claim payments would weaken a differential-rate explanation, without excluding opportunity inequity.

**Readiness:** Consistent annual-payment associations; mechanisms untested in current register. **Priority:** Separate physician stream. **Next step:** Keep annual-payment and per-service estimates separate. Obtain complete claims and measured clinical time; do not infer salary or patient coverage from receipts.

Motivating evidence: [L-C01; PMID 31926887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31926887/); [L-C02; PMID 34495296](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34495296/); [L-C03; PMID 39046733](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39046733/). Counterevidence or qualification: [L-C02; PMID 34495296](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34495296/); [L-C03; PMID 39046733](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39046733/).

### HYP-07. Policy expansion and lymphedema burden

**Patient care/access. Hypothesis:** The 2024 Medicare compression-item benefit reduces cost-related unmet lymphedema care, with absolute benefit varying by clinical burden and prior coverage.

**Population:** Medicare beneficiaries with clinically assessed lymphedema before and after implementation; breast and other causes, women and men.

**Exposure and comparison:** Effective access to the new compression-item benefit, distinct from pumps or surgical coverage. Comparable beneficiaries with differing prior coverage or a defensible unaffected comparison, assessed before and after implementation.

**Outcome:** Primary: cost-related unmet need for appropriate compression. Secondary: patient spending, receipt of prescribed items, symptoms and function.

**Proposed test:** Use repeated patient reports and claims in an event study with an explicit comparator, pretrend checks and implementation/supplier measures. Analyze sex and disease burden separately.

**Alternative explanations:** Supplier availability, take-up, coding changes, concurrent benefits, inflation, prior supplemental insurance and secular trends.

**What would weaken it:** A sufficiently precise estimate excluding a prespecified meaningful reduction in cost-related unmet need after verified implementation would weaken the access hypothesis. An imprecise null is inconclusive, and a claims increase alone would not confirm clinical benefit.

**Readiness:** Historical burden evidence and policy change identified; post-change outcomes needed. **Priority:** Requires new data. **Next step:** Verify implementation details and extract pre/post outcome data. Do not use 2023 insurer coverage rates as present-day Medicare compression coverage.

Motivating evidence: [L-A01; PMID 31446591](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31446591/); [L-A02; PMID 37285151](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37285151/). Policy/data: [P-021](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/lymphedema-compression-treatment-items); [P-022](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/regulations-guidance/transmittals/2024-transmittals/r12532bp).

### HYP-08. Complexity and breast reconstruction valuation

**Service/anatomy valuation. Hypothesis:** Time-intensive breast reconstruction receives lower valuation relative to complete episode work than simpler reconstruction, beyond any similar pattern in other reconstructive services.

**Population:** Clinically defined flap and implant breast reconstruction, plus defensible non-breast reconstructive comparison groups.

**Exposure and comparison:** Procedure complexity and complete episode work; breast anatomy is shared, and physician gender is not the exposure. Flap versus implant reconstruction first; then compare that contrast with analogous complexity contrasts outside breast care.

**Outcome:** Primary: work RVUs relative to independently measured full episode work. Secondary: standardized real payment trends, analyzed separately from the complexity contrast.

**Proposed test:** Combine current valuations with validated pre-, intra- and postoperative work measures. Account for technique, laterality, concurrent procedures, complications, global services and setting. Estimate time trends separately from cross-procedure levels.

**Alternative explanations:** Bundling, excluded postoperative work, patient selection, different risk/intensity and a general valuation problem affecting time-intensive surgery.

**What would weaken it:** Removal of the gap after full episode measurement would weaken operative-time-only interpretations. Similar gaps outside breast care would challenge a breast-specific excess, though a broader complexity problem could remain.

**Readiness:** Within-breast associations available; complete work and outside comparators needed. **Priority:** Secondary valuation study. **Next step:** Extract reconstruction studies in full and define independent episode work. A later patient-access test requires linked data on offers, delays and nonrecipients; the initial valuation test cannot establish those effects.

Motivating evidence: [L-P07; PMID 42085599](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42085599/); [L-P08; PMID 41701182](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41701182/); [L-P09; PMID 32453268](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32453268/); [L-P04; PMID 34237766](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34237766/). Policy/data: [P-035](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/fee-schedules/physician/pfs-relative-value-files/rvu26c).

### HYP-09. Medication affordability and sex differences in nonadherence

**Patient care/access. Hypothesis:** Among beneficiaries with comparable medication needs, financial burden contributes to sex differences in cost-related nonadherence, with potentially different effects for younger and older beneficiaries.

**Population:** Medicare beneficiaries using indicated medicines; retain <65 and >=65 strata and resolve the project identity criterion before primary inclusion.

**Exposure and comparison:** Actual patient cost sharing and changes in financial protection; keep coverage, income and disease burden distinct. Comparable medication needs across sex and policy periods; benefit-change studies need defensible controls.

**Outcome:** Annual any cost-related nonadherence and longitudinal persistence analyzed separately; retain reduced spending on necessities as a separate outcome.

**Proposed test:** Extract complete age-specific estimates, assess overlap and outcome compatibility, then examine policy-change designs with pretrends, clinical need and potential mediators specified.

**Alternative explanations:** Different illness and medicine burdens, income, recall, patient preferences, plan selection and survivor selection; adjustment may also remove pathways of disparity.

**What would weaken it:** Precise null contrasts in clinically comparable groups and no differential response to financial protection would weaken the proposed mechanism; imprecise null results do not establish equality.

**Readiness:** Two provisional full-text candidates; identity eligibility, human verification and appraisal pending. **Priority:** Further evidence extraction. **Next step:** Verify complete tables and model denominators, retrieve supplements, complete independent human review and search for compatible policy-change studies.

Motivating evidence: [L-R01; PMID 33298705](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33298705/). Counterevidence or qualification: [L-R02; PMID 33656528](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33656528/).

## Interpretation and design limits

- These hypotheses were generated after inspecting the selected evidence. They are exploratory, not prospectively registered confirmatory tests. Prespecify the next analyses, effect sizes of interest and matching rules before testing.
- The 40 care rows and 38 literature records are a selected starting set, with 31 primary candidates and 7 context records. They are not all Medicare services or an unbiased sample of disparities.
- Formal risk-of-bias appraisal, complete searches and full-text eligibility remain incomplete. Reporting male/female does not establish cisgender identity. Apply the current population criterion without relabeling cohorts.
- Distinguish sex/gender, anatomy, need, disease burden and physician gender. Clinical need must be defined independently of coverage and observed utilization. Include eligible people who did not receive care.
- Preserve original Medicare, MA, Part D, contractor, plan, year, setting and age/entitlement distinctions. Do not pool patient, physician and procedure-code effects.
- Retain null and reversed findings. Different clinical benefit or risk may justify different rules; equal rules may still create unequal burdens. Neither difference nor parity alone establishes fairness.
- For causal work, specify whether income, clinical time, referrals and service mix are confounders or mechanisms. Report total and pathway-specific estimates separately; mediation assumptions require justification.
- CMS formularies describe 5,499 plan segments using 328 formularies, not 5,499 independent decisions or beneficiary-weighted exposure. No denials, paid amounts, clinical indications or unmet-need denominators are present.
- Lower spending can reflect efficient care, lower need, different service mix or unmet care. Spending per treated beneficiary is not patient out-of-pocket cost. A nonsignificant estimate with a wide interval is not equivalence.
