# Investigating whether Medicare meets the care needs of women and men fairly

Draft press concept | September 16, 2026 | Review in progress

This project examines whether Medicare coverage, payments and patient costs support fair access to needed care for women and men. It looks beyond whether a treatment appears on a covered-services list to whether patients can afford it and obtain appropriate care.

The review separates differences involving patients from differences involving physicians. It also distinguishes care for shared body systems, such as bones and the heart, from care linked to reproductive anatomy or physiology. The current phase focuses on cisgender women and men, including younger Medicare beneficiaries with disabilities.

A **systematic literature review** uses a documented search and consistent rules to find, assess and bring together relevant research. It examines supporting evidence, conflicting results and gaps in knowledge. Two established approaches help explain how this work will be reported and assessed:

- **PRISMA makes the review transparent.** It guides reporting of where researchers searched, which studies they included, which they excluded and why, and how they reached their conclusions. A flow diagram shows the path from search results to included studies. PRISMA is a reporting guideline; following its checklist does not by itself prove that a review is reliable. [M-001; PMID 33782057](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33782057/)
- **GRADE explains how much confidence to place in a finding.** For questions where it applies, it assesses the body of evidence for each important outcome using four levels: high, moderate, low or very low certainty. It considers study limitations, agreement between results, relevance to the question, precision and potentially missing evidence. This is not a single score assigned to every paper. [M-002; PMID 21195583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21195583/)

For this Medicare project, those distinctions matter. A payment difference can be measured even when its cause remains uncertain. A coverage rule can be verified even when its effect on patients has not been established. Official policy documents and research about their effects will therefore be assessed separately.

The goal is to identify where the evidence supports concern, where it shows similar treatment, and where better research is needed. The project has assembled a preliminary evidence register and developed hypotheses. Comprehensive searching, full screening, formal study appraisal and outcome-specific certainty assessments remain ahead. **No completed PRISMA-reported systematic review or formal GRADE ratings are being announced at this stage.**

PRISMA stands for *Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses*. GRADE stands for *Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation*.

Methodology references, including PMIDs, are retained separately in the [living bibliography](./Medicare_gender_care_bibliography.md#methodology-and-reporting-guidance). They are not additional Medicare outcome studies. Official guidance: [PRISMA 2020](https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020) and the [GRADE Working Group](https://www.gradeworkinggroup.org/).
