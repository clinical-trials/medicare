# Medicare reimbursement review: national question and population scope

Scope amendment adopted September 16, 2026, after the initial broad literature search, at the user's direction. This scope applies to subsequent evidence extraction, the systematic literature review, and the future white paper unless the user revises it.

## Review question

Across Medicare, how do coverage rules, payment values, cost sharing and administrative requirements affect access to clinically indicated care for cisgender women and men? What evidence shows systematic sex-based inequities, including unequal effects of rules that apply equally on paper?

Estradiol is a motivating example within this national inquiry. The review tests whether care needed by women is systematically disadvantaged, while retaining evidence of parity, differences affecting men, and uncertainty. A finding about a single drug cannot establish or resolve the national question.

## Why Medicare is the benchmark

Clarified September 16, 2026 at the user's direction. Medicare is the national policy benchmark for evaluating equitable access to clinically indicated care in later life, when chronic illness and per-person healthcare use are substantial. It is a payer-policy benchmark, not a definition of clinically appropriate care or a representative sample of all US residents. The benchmark rationale and verified sources are maintained in `research/medicare_benchmark.json`.

CMS reports that adults 65+ represented 17% of the population and 37% of all-payer personal health care spending in 2020. Per-person spending was $22,356 for ages 65+ versus $9,154 for ages 19–64. NCHS estimates of 2019 office-based physician visits also show higher per-person use among older adults than younger adults. These measures support the later-life emphasis. They do not demonstrate that most medical encounters or most lifetime care occur after 65, and the spending estimates do not isolate pandemic effects.

The age-specific spending denominator is all-payer personal health care, whereas Medicare's 21% share in 2024 refers to national health expenditures. Keep spending, utilization, unmet need and clinical benefit separate. Do not attribute observed age differences solely to biological aging or extrapolate Medicare findings to all ages and payers without supporting evidence.

Retain eligible younger Medicare beneficiaries; this clarification does not impose a new age exclusion. When evidence permits, stratify ages 65–74, 75–84 and 85+, identify under-65 beneficiaries and entitlement through disability, ESRD or ALS, and preserve mixed-age studies with limitations. For sex comparisons, retain age, clinical need, follow-up/person-time and mortality information. Use age standardization or stratification when justified and prespecify other adjustments rather than automatically removing mechanisms of inequity.

## National analytic scope

- Examine prescription drugs, procedures, preventive services, diagnostic pathways, rehabilitation and other covered benefits. Include sex-specific conditions, conditions predominantly affecting one sex, and shared conditions with sex differences in need, treatment or access. The scope extends beyond reproductive and sexual health to areas such as cardiovascular care, cancer, musculoskeletal disease, pain and mental health.
- Distinguish four outcomes: benefit eligibility and coverage restrictions; allowed payments and relative value units; patient spending; and realized access, including denials, delays and unmet need. Formulary listing, payment to a clinician and receipt of appropriate care are different measures.
- Separate Original Medicare, Medicare Advantage and Part D. Identify national rules, contractor decisions and plan implementation. Preserve policy year, geographic scope and denominators; describe plan samples and enrollment weighting before drawing national conclusions.
- Use within-condition sex comparisons, clinically justified comparisons of service groups, and assessments of population burden under the same rules. Do not require a one-to-one male counterpart for every women's health service. Where no counterpart exists, assess clinical need, benefit, alternatives and consistency of policy rationale.
- Assess explicit sex distinctions and disproportionate effects of formally neutral policies. Differences in utilization, service mix and benefit design can be mechanisms as well as potential confounders; specify their role before adjustment rather than automatically removing their effects.
- Distinguish observed differences, unmet clinical need and evidence supporting structural inequity. A disparity alone does not identify its cause. The current inventory is a selected starting set, not a census of all Medicare services or a completed systematic review.

## Clinical anatomy and physiology distinction

Added September 16, 2026 at the user's request. Classify every care-item/indication using `research/clinical_basis.json`: shared body systems/functions; female reproductive anatomy; male reproductive anatomy; female reproductive physiology; male reproductive physiology; or mixed/site-dependent services. These describe the clinical service, not a person's gender identity.

Record sex-defined eligibility, physiology and population burden separately. A women-specific drug label or screening rule does not make the treated organ or function exclusive to women. Breast, bone, lymphatic and pelvic-floor care can concern shared systems. Menopause is a reproductive physiological context even when therapy acts systemically. Hormone names alone do not determine classification. Local vaginal treatment and systemic menopausal treatment have different clinical bases. These distinctions are supported by [L-B01] and clinical guidance sources C-001–C-004 in the bibliography.

For shared conditions, compare women and men with comparable clinical need within the same service, indication and setting, including those who do not receive care. Assess clinical eligibility independently of reimbursement rules so that excluded or unmet need is not removed from the analysis. Sex-defined clinical indications can instead be assessed within the eligible population and against appropriate alternatives; do not force an off-label opposite-sex comparator. For reproductive anatomy or physiology, assess clinical need, benefits, alternatives and policy consistency; use cross-anatomy procedure comparisons only with a defensible match of purpose and resources. Do not require an opposite-sex counterpart. Keep the two types of evidence separate in the white paper.

Split bundled or site-dependent rows before comparative synthesis. Sling procedures, broad pelvic-pain care, fertility services and endometriosis operations cannot all be assigned to a single sex or organ without component detail. The broad prolapse row remains provisional until its organ/site and procedure codes are verified. Systemic estradiol plus progestogen is classified primarily by menopausal physiology while retaining the endometrial-protection requirement.

Dashboard counts summarize the selected inventory rows, not unique codes, patient prevalence, nationwide coverage rates or the frequency of bias. Do not pool category counts or raw spending as a causal test of reimbursement inequity. Population burden and reproductive physiology may affect the need for shared-system care; retain both features in interpretation.

## Population criterion

- Focus on cisgender men and women. Exclude transgender-focused patient studies and gender-affirming-care indications from the present synthesis.
- For mixed populations, extract only separately reported eligible cisgender male/female results. Do not include combined results that cannot be separated in the primary synthesis. Record the exclusion reason.
- A study that reports only male/female does not establish participants' gender identity. Retain it as candidate evidence pending full-text eligibility review, record identity as “not reported,” and do not claim that transgender participants were definitely absent. Seek clarification where feasible. Resolve uncertain eligibility before the final synthesis.
- Do not infer gender identity from anatomy, diagnosis, medication, recorded sex, or clinician assumptions. Sexual orientation and intersex status alone are not additional exclusion criteria established by this amendment.
- Medicare policy, formulary and procedure-code data have no individual patient population. They remain relevant for included indications, but cannot demonstrate patient-level exclusion or a cisgender-only outcome estimate.

## Reporting rules for the white paper

- State the target population and this dated scope amendment in Methods. Do not describe an amendment made after initial searching as a prospectively registered criterion.
- Preserve the original publications' terms for sex and gender and describe how each was measured. Do not relabel sex-coded cohorts as confirmed cisgender populations.
- Separate patient studies from code-level valuations, benefit policy and formulary analyses. Separate current coverage from historical access findings, and Medicare evidence from other-payer evidence.
- Clinician-gender papers supplied as research leads may be retained as clearly labeled contextual literature. They remain outside the primary patient-sex disparity synthesis. Collections, physician salary, reimbursement per identical service and coverage for patients are distinct outcomes.
- Broad clinical reviews may support the rationale, clinical framework and citation following. Label them as background; extract their primary references separately before using those references as direct reimbursement evidence. Apply the population scope to each underlying patient study rather than labeling a general review cisgender-only.
- Original qualitative interviews with clinicians may be retained as contextual evidence about patient-access mechanisms. Distinguish reported perceptions from measured patient outcomes and Medicare-specific effects. Appraise recruitment, analytic methods and treatment of contradictory accounts; do not categorize these studies as clinician-pay comparisons or narrative reviews.
- Retain relevant null and contradictory findings. A reimbursement difference or coverage gap alone does not establish causal bias.
- Report exclusion reasons and final eligibility decisions. Counts in the working register are not a complete systematic-search flow diagram.

The current artifact remains a preliminary evidence register. Comprehensive reproducible searches, full-text screening, eligibility adjudication, risk-of-bias assessment and synthesis remain to be completed before calling it a completed systematic review.

## Citation and bibliography practice

Maintain a living bibliography with stable study IDs, verified publication metadata, DOI/PMID links, screening status, source-access level and linked corrections. Preserve earlier citations and excluded records for audit. Keep national policy, contractor and plan documents and data releases identifiable separately from scientific publications. Metadata verification does not imply full-text extraction, eligibility or endorsement of a finding. References cited by another review remain leads until independently retrieved and assessed. Use stable `[L-ID]` citation placeholders in drafts; assign publication numbering after the evidence set is finalized.
